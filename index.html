The book

Skip two years of trial and error. Keep the results.

Most companies will spend years and millions learning what ACME learned the hard way. This book hands you the whole playbook: what worked, what failed, the real numbers, and the management questions to settle before you spend a single euro. Read it in a weekend. Apply it on Monday.

2 years of real deployment, compressed into one read
16 chapters in two parts: building intelligence on your own terms, then the management reality check
8,500 h returned in one year: the ROI case for your board
No one replaced the goal is to free people for more valuable work

Why we wrote it

We believe responsible AI is not only decided in Brussels or in Washington. It is built, or not, inside companies, one decision at a time. We believe every organization must remain the owner of its own intelligence, and that AI, done well, does not replace human expertise: it reveals it. Some tasks, yes, will disappear. All the more reason to talk about it openly.

“What can I do, where I stand?” Camus and Arendt’s question is now every leader’s question in the face of AI.

The problem

Your employees did not wait for your AI strategy.

In the group we followed, 3,000 people were already using public AI before a single committee had ruled. Every day, a little of how the company works, reasons and decides risked leaving through the browser window. It was not shadow IT. It was shadow cognition.

Part I · Building Intelligence on Your Own Terms

The AI reality test. A documentary, not a manual.

Gregory Renard, an applied AI architect since 1996, takes you inside ACME, a multi-billion-dollar industrial group headquartered in Europe, from the first spark in San Francisco in 2024 to an internal platform used by thousands of employees. It is neither a retrospective case study nor a polished success story. It is an account written while the transformation was happening.

  • →Why you never ban public AI overnight: tolerate, redirect, then block.
  • →Fifty one-to-one conversations, a hundred use cases, fifteen priorities.
  • →The “Nicolas effect”: two days of work down to fifteen minutes, and an expert who became indispensable.
  • →The Squares, the Rounds and the patatoïde: when IT and AI finally make peace.
  • →The day the AI team said “not yet” to the most prestigious project.

Part II · Management Reality Check

The management reality check. A lab, not a lecture.

Marylene Delbourg-Delphis, a tech entrepreneur since the 1980s and a philosopher by training, steps back. You cannot exploit a new technological paradigm while leaving intact management assumptions inherited from other eras.

  • →Are you ready? The Innovation Readiness Ratio: landing gear ÷ amplitude, before you choose the thrust.
  • →Four operabilities to check: organizational, cognitive, temporal, normative.
  • →What to answer, honestly, when someone asks “Will AI replace me?”
  • →Which human, in which loop, with what authority? Why AI becomes political.
  • →Governing agentic AI with TRUST, and measuring time returned rather than time saved.

System of Intelligence · the idea behind the book

Your ERP runs your past. Who runs your intelligence?

Systems of record administer the company’s existence. Systems of intelligence are how it does business and competes. Does your company have one?

A system of intelligence is the layer that mobilizes your company’s own know-how, learns from your experts and stays under your control. It sits next to your ERP, CRM and HRIS.

System of record

System of intelligence

Stores structured data

Analyses, reasons and acts on knowledge

Administers the company’s existence

Is how it does business and competes

Same processes as your competitors

Captures what no competitor can buy

A vendor AI only sees its own silo

Crosses silos: documents, email, business lines

Wears out over time

Improves with every expert correction

FAQ

Questions

Which company is ACME? +

A large European industrial group that we call ACME. People’s first names are pseudonyms too: what matters is the process, not the identity.

Is this a technical book? +

No. Technical notions appear when they are needed, explained for non-specialists. The book connects the pieces without becoming a manual.

I do not work in a large industrial group. Is it for me? +

Yes. The story is less about ACME’s specifics than about what it takes to make AI work in an organization. You will read it as a documentary.

Was AI used to write or translate this book? +

The book was written by its two authors. The French edition was translated with the help of AI tools, then reviewed and approved sentence by sentence by the authors. For a book on responsible AI, we wanted to say so.

Which languages is the book available in? +

English and French, both as an ebook and an audiobook, released on the same day. The book was written in English; the French edition was translated and approved by the authors.

Who is the publisher? +

Freedom.ai, the authors’ own imprint. Publishing it ourselves lets us set the price, keep the rights, and release both languages on the same day.

Is there a print edition? +

Not at launch: ebook and audiobook only.

Can we invite the authors? +
